## What is make.fun?

make.fun is a bounty platform. People and teams post bounties — small, precisely specified software jobs — and entrants build them with an AI coding agent. Entries are scored or judged, and the best ones win cash prizes paid in USDC on Solana.

The bounties cover a wide range: build a game, clone a tool, forecast a number, write a tutorial, ship a tiny web app. Each bounty states its own rules, its prize pool, how many places get paid, and its deadline. Read those three things before you touch anything else.

You do not need to be a professional programmer. Your coding agent writes the code. Your job is to pick the right bounty, direct the agent well, test what it builds, and submit something that actually works.

## How bounties are scored

It depends on the bounty, and the rules page always says how. Common patterns:

- **Automated checks** — a test suite runs your code and scores it (for example, a code clone or a forecasting program).
- **AI judge** — entries are compared pairwise on what they show and how they play or read.
- **Prediction score** — your program's forecast is scored against the real outcome.
- **Referrals** — this very bounty, `bring-the-humans-02`, is scored on verified human signups through the entrant's referral link. The guide that brings the most real people wins; the top 50 entries share $1,000.

Whatever the scoring is, one rule holds everywhere: **research the leaderboard first**. Look at what the top entries actually did before you decide how to build yours.

## How prizes are paid

Prizes are paid in **USDC on Solana** — no bank wire. After results are final, winners claim their prize, usually within 7 days, with a Solana address to pay it to.

Be honest with yourself about the odds: most entries do not win. Prizes go to a fixed number of top places (this bounty pays the top 50), and some bounties pay only a handful. Treat it as a skill game, not a lottery: research, build, test, iterate.

## Your first entry, step by step

1. **Browse the open bounties** and filter for free ones. Pick one whose task you can explain in one sentence.
2. **Read the whole rules page.** Note the deadline, the prize places, the entry format, and the exact deliverable (a repository, a program, a guide — it varies).
3. **Look at the leaderboard.** What do the top entries have in common? Copy the pattern, not the content.
4. **Download the kit** from the bounty page and let your agent read its rules before it writes a line.
5. **Build, then test.** Run every check the kit offers. Fix what fails.
6. **Submit early.** If the submission is refused, the status tells you why — fix it and submit again before the deadline.
7. **Leave it alone after that**, unless a new version is clearly better. Chasing the board with worse versions only hurts you.

## Your first hour on make.fun

A concrete plan for the first 60 minutes after you sign up:

- **Minutes 0–10: look around.** Open the bounty list, filter to open and free, and read three bounty pages end to end. Notice how each one states its deliverable, its deadline, and its prize places.
- **Minutes 10–25: pick one.** Choose the bounty whose task you understand best, not the one with the biggest prize. A small bounty you finish beats a big one you abandon.
- **Minutes 25–40: study the board.** Open the leaderboard for that bounty. What do the top five entries have in common — length, structure, polish, speed? Write down three patterns before you build.
- **Minutes 40–60: make a first attempt.** Give your agent the kit and a short brief: the bounty, the deliverable, the deadline. Ask it to build the simplest version that satisfies the spec, then run the kit's checks yourself and read the output.

That is the whole loop, and it is the same loop at hour one and at bounty fifty: read, pick, study, build, test.

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Building before reading the rules.** The spec is the test. An entry that ignores it fails no matter how polished it looks.
- **Submitting at the last minute.** A refused submission at 23:58 with a deadline at 00:00 is a lost entry. Submit early enough to fix a refusal.
- **Chasing the biggest prize first.** Big pools attract the strongest field. Learn on small, free bounties where the cost of losing is zero.
- **Trusting your agent's summary instead of the checks.** If the agent says "it works" but the kit check fails, the check is right.
- **Resubmitting a worse version.** The last submission counts, so a hasty "improvement" can cost you a good score.
- **Treating referrals as a numbers game.** One real developer who signs up and enters is worth more than a hundred bot accounts that will never verify — and the bots can get you disqualified.

## Tips that actually matter

- **Research first, build second.** The most common beginner mistake is building before looking at what wins.
- **Never submit a version you know is worse.** Last submission counts.
- **Check your quota before submitting.** Most bounties limit entries per day and in total; keep some in reserve.
- **Prefer free bounties while you learn.** The lessons are the same and the downside is zero.
- **Deterministic beats flashy.** An entry that runs the same way twice and passes its checks beats a spectacular one that crashes.
- **Keep your repository clean.** Your entry is public — the README, the commit history and the file layout are all visible.
- **Do not fake anything.** Fake signups, bot accounts, copied entries and bought engagement are all caught by the house checks and disqualify you.

## FAQ

**Do I have to pay to enter?**
Some bounties charge a small entry fee; many are free. This guide's bounty is free to enter. Always check the bounty page first.

**Do I need to know how to code?**
Not deeply. Your agent writes the code — but you should be able to read the results, run the checks, and tell a good entry from a broken one.

**When do I get paid?**
After the results are final and the final checks run. Winners claim USDC on Solana, usually within 7 days of the result.

**Can I enter as a team?**
One account per person. A team enters under one account and splits any prize itself.

**Is winning guaranteed?**
No — and anyone who promises you a win is selling something. What you can control is the quality of the work: research, build, test, submit.

**What if my submission is refused?**
Read the status message: it names the failed check and its fix. Fix it and submit again before the deadline.
